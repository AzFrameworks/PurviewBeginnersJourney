# Chapter 9: Data Map and Unified Catalog

So far, much of our journey has followed files, messages, users, and activities. Purview also governs structured and analytical data.

Data Map is the technical metadata foundation. It captures metadata from analytics, SaaS, operational, hybrid, on-premises, and multicloud systems. Scanning and lineage ingestion populate information about assets, schemas, classifications, owners, relationships, and data movement. The map stores metadata rather than copying the underlying business data.

[Learn about Microsoft Purview Data Map](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/data-map)

[Plan for data governance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/data-governance-plan)

Unified Catalog is the business-facing layer built on that metadata. Data stewards add business meaning through governance domains, glossary terms, owners, critical data elements, data products, quality information, and terms of use. Data consumers can search for governed assets and data products rather than depending solely on tribal knowledge or technical table names.

[Search for data assets in the Microsoft Purview Unified Catalog](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/unified-catalog-data-assets-search)

[Data products in the Microsoft Purview Unified Catalog](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/unified-catalog-data-products)

Here is a practical analogy. Data Map is the technical map of every road, pipe, and building. Unified Catalog is the visitor guide that explains what those places are for, who owns them, whether they are trustworthy, and how to request access.

This distinction also explains why governance requires both technical and business participation. Technical teams can reveal where data comes from and how it moves, while business owners explain its meaning, quality, permitted use, and value. The catalog becomes useful when those perspectives meet and people can confidently decide whether a data product is suitable for their purpose.

For example, an analyst may search for a trusted customer dataset. Data Map can show where the asset lives and how it feeds a report. Unified Catalog can explain its business meaning, owner, quality, and approved use. The analyst can make a better choice without relying on a colleague’s memory.

Microsoft Fabric is especially relevant here. Fabric assets can appear in governance and labeling experiences, while lineage helps explain how source data becomes a semantic model, report, or analytical product. Purview can also govern supported interactions with Copilot in Fabric, including classification, audit, lifecycle, communication-compliance, and regulatory-assessment scenarios.

[Learn about sensitivity labels](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/sensitivity-labels),

[Use Microsoft Purview to manage Microsoft Copilot in Fabric](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/ai-copilot-fabric)
