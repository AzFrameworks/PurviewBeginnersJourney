# Chapter 5: When Risk Depends on Context

![Purview Introduction Chapter 5](pics/PurviewIntroductionChapter5.png)

Not every risky event means the same thing.

A single file download may be routine. Thousands of unusual downloads followed by uploads to an unapproved service may deserve attention. Insider Risk Management correlates signals to help identify potentially malicious or inadvertent internal risks, including data leakage, intellectual-property theft, and security-policy violations. It supports policies, alerts, investigation, cases, and escalation to eDiscovery where appropriate.

Privacy is central to this area. Microsoft documents pseudonymization by default, role-based controls, explicit administrative opt-in for indicators, and audit logging. Organizations still need their own legal, employee-relations, works-council, and privacy review. A risk signal is not proof of intent or misconduct. It is a reason for an appropriately authorized team to examine the facts.

Adaptive Protection connects detected risk to dynamic controls. Instead of applying the strictest rule to everyone, it can use insider-risk levels to adjust DLP, Data Lifecycle Management, and Microsoft Entra Conditional Access controls. When a user’s risk context changes, the corresponding controls can change as well.

Think of it as adaptive traffic control. Normal traffic flows normally. When conditions indicate elevated risk, additional controls appear where they are most relevant.

The important distinction is between an event and a pattern. Context such as timing, volume, destination, role, and previous activity can change how an event should be understood. Responsible use therefore depends on carefully designed policies, limited access to cases, and a review process that considers both business need and individual privacy.

For example, a designer may download many large files before a business trip. That may be expected. The same activity from an unfamiliar device followed by uploads to an unapproved service may require review. The activity is similar. The surrounding context changes its meaning.
