## Chapter 8: Managing Information over Time

Data that is never deleted becomes a risk. Data deleted too early can create legal, operational, or regulatory problems.

Data Lifecycle Management applies retention and deletion requirements to supported content. Retention policies can apply broadly to locations or populations. Retention labels can express more specific rules for individual items or categories.

Records Management builds on retention concepts for information that must be treated as an official business record. It supports capabilities such as file plans, record declaration, event-based retention, and disposition review. The distinction is helpful: lifecycle management answers how long should this content exist? Records management adds is this an authoritative record, and what controlled process governs its final disposition?

[Implement retention, eDiscovery, and Communication Compliance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/purview-implement-retention-ediscovery-communication-compliance/)

[Create and manage an eDiscovery hold](https://learn.microsoft.com/purview/edisc-hold-manage)

Compliance Manager takes a wider regulatory view. It provides assessments, controls, improvement actions, evidence management, workflow, and a risk-based compliance score. Microsoft-managed, customer-managed, and shared controls reflect the cloud shared-responsibility model. The score can help prioritize work, but it is not a legal certification or a guarantee of compliance.

Lifecycle decisions are ultimately business decisions expressed through technology. Teams need to agree which information has lasting value, which obligations require preservation, and when continued storage creates unnecessary risk. A practical program starts with the most important content categories and assigns clear owners for approving exceptions and final disposition.

For example, routine collaboration drafts may be deleted after a short period. Signed contracts may need to remain much longer. A legal hold may pause normal deletion for a specific matter. The right outcome depends on the purpose of the information, not only on where it is stored.

[Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/compliance-manager)

[Working with improvement actions in Compliance Manager](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/compliance-manager-improvement-actions)

Two additional controls answer narrower but important questions. Information Barriers can restrict communication and collaboration between designated groups in Teams, SharePoint, and OneDrive, often to address conflicts of interest or regulatory separation. Privileged Access Management limits standing administrative access to sensitive Exchange tasks through just-in-time access rules.
